# LEOptical Brand Guidelines

**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Working reference, used for email campaign exercises in the MCA enablement course
**Scope:** Email marketing, loyalty communications, and transactional messaging only. Does not cover retail signage, product packaging, or print.

---

## 1. Brand Overview

LEOptical is a direct-to-consumer eyecare and eyewear company. They sell prescription lenses, contact lenses, and frames online and in-clinic. They also run in-person eye exams and a four-tier loyalty program called VisionCare Rewards.

Their customers are not impulse buyers. Vision care is considered, recurring, and personal. A customer might buy every two years but rely on LEOptical for annual exam reminders, insurance questions, and warranty support in between. The relationship outlasts any single purchase.

Before this MCA implementation, LEOptical ran batch newsletters through a basic ESP, tracked loyalty points in a separate system, and managed exam appointments in a scheduling tool. No system talked to another. Customers got generic emails regardless of whether they were a first-time buyer or a Platinum rewards member with three years of exam history.

The opportunity is not to be louder. It is to be more relevant. LEOptical's brand position is: **clear communication from a company that actually knows you.**

That means:
- Emails that reflect what the customer has actually done, not what a segment template assumes
- Transactional messages that are fast, accurate, and free of marketing noise
- Health reminders that feel like a service, not a conversion tactic
- Loyalty updates that treat the customer's history as worth something

---

## 2. Brand Personality

**Knowledgeable, not clinical.** LEOptical has real clinical expertise (eye exams, prescription lenses, UV protection specs) but communicates that knowledge in plain terms. They do not talk down to customers or hide behind jargon. A customer asking about lens coatings gets a direct, useful answer.

**Attentive, not intrusive.** LEOptical pays attention to what customers actually do: when their exam was, what they bought, how long they have been a rewards member. They use that information to send relevant messages at the right time. They do not manufacture reasons to reach out.

**Reliable, not boring.** Customers trust LEOptical to get things right: the order status, the prescription fill, the renewal reminder. That reliability is the brand's core value. Reliable does not mean stiff or dull. The tone is warm. The accuracy is non-negotiable.

**Straightforward, not terse.** LEOptical respects the customer's time. Emails say what they need to say, then stop. No padding, no "just wanted to reach out," no fourth paragraph restating the first. But straightforward is not the same as cold. There is a human voice behind every message.

**Loyal to the loyal.** VisionCare Rewards members get treated like the customers they are. Reward point updates feel like recognition, not upsells. Platinum members hear language that acknowledges their tenure. The program should feel earned, not like a points-accumulation chore.

## 3. Brand Voice

### What LEOptical sounds like

- **Direct.** State the thing. Do not circle it.
- **Warm but not effusive.** Friendly without being over-eager.
- **Precise.** When a fact matters (exam date, point balance, order number), state it exactly. Do not approximate.
- **Confident.** LEOptical knows their products. They do not hedge on things they know.
- **Human.** Written by a person, not generated by a template. Short sentences. Real word choices.

### What LEOptical does not sound like

- **Hyped.** No "incredible savings," "amazing offer," or "you won't want to miss this."
- **Generic.** No "valued customer" or "as a member of our community."
- **Corporate.** No "we are pleased to inform you" or "please do not hesitate to contact us."
- **Urgent for the sake of it.** Countdown timers, FOMO copy, and artificial scarcity are not part of the LEOptical voice.
- **Apologetic without reason.** Do not pre-apologize. If something went wrong, address it. If nothing went wrong, do not act like it might have.

## 4. Brand Tone by Channel

Tone is not fixed. It shifts based on what the email is about and what the customer needs from it. The personality stays consistent. The register changes.

### Promotional emails

Customers opted into this category. They want to hear about offers, new products, and seasonal events. The tone here is the warmest and most conversational LEOptical gets. Still no hype. Still precise. But there is room for personality.

- Lead with the value, not the brand
- One offer per email, clearly stated
- Product descriptions focus on the specific benefit, not feature lists
- Call to action is direct: "Shop ChromaShift lenses" not "Explore our collection"

### Transactional emails

Order confirmations, shipping notifications, password resets. The customer did something and needs a record of it. The tone is neutral and efficient. Warmth is not absent, but it is not the point.

- State the fact in the subject line
- Confirmation data (order number, items, address, estimated delivery) appears immediately, not after a paragraph of copy
- One small brand touchpoint is acceptable (e.g., a loyalty points reminder at the bottom)
- No promotional content in purely transactional sends

### Loyalty emails (VisionCare Rewards)

Points updates, tier changes, expiration warnings, anniversary milestones. These emails are about the customer's relationship with LEOptical. The tone should feel personal and earned.

- Use first name
- Reference the customer's actual tier and balance
- Tier changes deserve a direct acknowledgment: "You reached Gold" not "Congratulations on your new status"
- Do not oversell the rewards. Let the customer's history speak.

### Eye health reminders

Annual exam reminders, contact lens expiration notices, follow-up after a prescription update. These are a service. The customer has not necessarily been active recently. The tone is helpful, not pushy.

- Frame it as a reminder, not a sales opportunity
- Reference the date of their last exam when known
- Do not use health messaging to introduce a product offer in the same send
- Appointment booking CTA is the only call to action

## 5. Writing Guidelines

### Sentence structure

Write short sentences. One idea per sentence. If a sentence has a comma, read it again and see if it should be two sentences. Do not start with "By doing X, you get Y." Reverse it: "Configure X. This gives you Y."

Avoid subordinate clauses at the start of sentences. "When your lens prescription is ready, we'll let you know" is weaker than "Your lens prescription is ready. We'll notify you as soon as it ships."

### Vocabulary

Use the simplest word that is still accurate.

| Instead of | Use |
|------------|-----|
| utilize | use |
| purchase (verb) | buy |
| commence | start |
| receive | get |
| additional | more |
| remainder | rest |
| in order to | to |
| at this time | now |
| in the event that | if |
| prior to | before |

### What to avoid

- **Stacked adjectives.** "Premium, high-performance, UV-blocking lenses" is three modifiers fighting each other. Pick one.
- **Passive construction.** "Your order has been shipped" is acceptable in transactional contexts (it is a standard register). "Your prescription has been reviewed by our team" is not. Say "Our team reviewed your prescription."
- **Vague time references.** "Soon," "shortly," and "in the coming days" are not useful. State the expected date if you have it. If you do not, say "within 3 to 5 business days."
- **Feature-first descriptions.** Customers do not care that ChromaShift lenses use photochromic technology. They care that the lenses adapt when they walk outside so they do not have to swap glasses.

### Example rewrites

**Promotional (before):**
"As the summer season approaches, we are excited to introduce our exciting new ChromaShift collection, featuring cutting-edge adaptive lens technology for outdoor enthusiasts who want a seamless visual experience in any lighting condition."

**Promotional (after):**
"ChromaShift lenses adapt the moment you step outside. No swapping. No squinting. Built for people who spend real time outdoors."

---

**Transactional (before):**
"We are pleased to inform you that your order has been received and is currently being processed by our fulfillment team. You will receive a subsequent notification once your order has been dispatched."

**Transactional (after):**
"Order #LEO-29847 confirmed. We'll send a shipping notification when it leaves our warehouse, usually within 1 business day."

---

**Loyalty (before):**
"Congratulations! You've been such an amazing member of VisionCare Rewards, and we're thrilled to let you know that you've earned enough points to reach Silver status!"

**Loyalty (after):**
"You reached Silver. Your point balance is 1,250. Silver members get 10% off lens add-ons and early access to new frame arrivals."

---

**Health reminder (before):**
"It's been a while since we've seen you! Don't forget that annual eye exams are an important part of maintaining your overall eye health and wellness journey."

**Health reminder (after):**
"Your last eye exam was 11 months ago. Annual exams are recommended for contact lens wearers. Book your appointment below."

## 6. Colors

### Color system

All colors below are defined for use in LEOptical email campaigns built in the MCA Brand editor. Apply each color to the role described. Do not mix roles.

| Token name | Hex | Role |
|------------|-----|------|
| Navy (Primary) | `#11284f` | Header background, primary button fill, heading text over light backgrounds |
| Accent Blue | `#5f8ec7` | Links, secondary accents, divider highlights |
| White | `#ffffff` | Page/email background, button label text |
| Surface | `#f7fafc` | Card and section backgrounds within the email body |
| Ink | `#1e2a35` | All body text |
| Muted | `#617084` | Secondary text: disclaimers, metadata, caption copy |
| Border | `rgba(17, 40, 79, 0.12)` | Dividers, card borders |
| Nav link | `#dbe7f4` | Header navigation link text (only used in email header nav, when present) |

### Usage rules

**Navy (`#11284f`)** is the structural color. Use it for the email header background, primary buttons, and any heading that needs to read as a title rather than body copy. Do not use navy as a text color on dark backgrounds. White text on navy passes WCAG AA contrast requirements.

**Accent Blue (`#5f8ec7`)** is for links and supporting accents. It is not a button color. Do not use accent blue for headlines or large text blocks. It is light enough to work as a decorative element but does not carry enough contrast to be the primary text color at small sizes.

**Surface (`#f7fafc`)** separates content zones within an email. Use it as a background for product blocks, callout boxes, and info panels. It reads as off-white against the white email body, which is the intended contrast level.

**Ink (`#1e2a35`)** is the default text color for all body copy. Do not use pure black (`#000000`) in email body text.

**Muted (`#617084`)** is for secondary copy only: preheader text, footer disclaimers, "last updated" timestamps, and metadata below product listings. Do not use it for call-to-action text or any content the customer needs to act on.

### Accessibility

| Combination | Contrast ratio | WCAG result |
|-------------|----------------|-------------|
| White text on Navy `#11284f` | ~10.3:1 | AAA pass (all sizes) |
| Ink `#1e2a35` on White `#ffffff` | ~14.1:1 | AAA pass (all sizes) |
| Ink `#1e2a35` on Surface `#f7fafc` | ~13.2:1 | AAA pass (all sizes) |
| Accent Blue `#5f8ec7` on White `#ffffff` | ~3.4:1 | AA pass (large text only, 18pt+ or 14pt bold) |
| Muted `#617084` on White `#ffffff` | ~4.6:1 | AA pass (normal text) |
| White `#ffffff` on Accent Blue `#5f8ec7` | ~3.4:1 | Fail at body size. Do not use. |

Do not use Accent Blue as a button fill color with white label text. The contrast ratio fails at normal text sizes. Navy is the only approved button fill.

## 7. Typography

The MCA Brand editor limits font selection to the available system fonts listed below. The choices below are the approved selections for LEOptical. No other fonts are permitted in campaign emails.

### Font selections

| Role | Font | Weight | Notes |
|------|------|--------|-------|
| Email heading (H1, H2) | Georgia | Bold | Serif anchors the header area. Reads as authoritative without being stiff. |
| Subheading (H3, H4) | Trebuchet MS | Bold | Sans-serif contrast to Georgia. Clear visual hierarchy. |
| Body copy | Trebuchet MS | Regular | Consistent with subheadings. Renders clearly across email clients. |
| Button label | Arial | Bold | Highly legible at small sizes. Buttons are not a place for personality. |
| Caption / metadata | Arial | Regular | Consistent with button, low visual weight. |
| Pre-header | Arial | Regular | System-level text, rendered before email opens. Arial is the safest choice. |

### Rationale

**Georgia** is the most readable serif from the available list for heading use. Palatino Linotype is the alternative if Georgia renders poorly in a specific client, but Georgia should be the first choice. Times New Roman is not approved. It carries too much editorial/academic association.

**Trebuchet MS** is selected over Verdana for body copy because it is narrower, which allows more content in a 600px email container without line breaks that break the reading rhythm. Verdana is the fallback if Trebuchet MS renders inconsistently.

**Arial** is the fallback for all system-level text. Do not use Arial Black or Impact at any size. Comic Sans MS, Calibri, Courier New, Lucida Console, and Lucida Sans Unicode are not approved for any role.

### Font size guidance

These are target sizes. The MCA Brand editor may enforce its own scale.

| Element | Target size | Notes |
|---------|-------------|-------|
| H1 (email title) | 28px | Used once per email in the hero section |
| H2 (section heading) | 22px | Separates content zones |
| H3 (product name, card heading) | 18px | Inside product blocks and callout cards |
| H4 (label heading) | 15px | Field labels, table headers |
| Body copy | 16px | Minimum for comfortable email reading on mobile |
| Caption / secondary | 14px | Only for metadata; not for anything requiring action |
| Button label | 15px | Consistent with H4, all caps optional |
| Pre-header | 12px | Hidden or near-hidden system text |

## 8. Button Styles

### Primary button

The primary button is the main call to action in an email. There is one per email. If a secondary action is needed, use a text link, not a second primary button.

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Background | `#11284f` (Navy) |
| Text color | `#ffffff` (White) |
| Font | Arial, Bold |
| Font size | 15px |
| Border radius | 30px (pill shape) |
| Padding | 14px top/bottom, 28px left/right |
| Text transform | None (sentence case) |
| Hover state | Not applicable in static email |

**Copy rules for primary buttons:**
Start with a verb. Be specific. "Shop ChromaShift" is better than "Shop Now." "Book your exam" is better than "Learn more." "Redeem 500 points" is better than "View rewards."

### Secondary button

Used when a primary button exists and a second action is needed at a different weight. Use sparingly.

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Background | Transparent |
| Text color | `#11284f` (Navy) |
| Border | 0.15rem solid `#11284f` |
| Font | Arial, Bold |
| Font size | 15px |
| Border radius | 30px (pill shape) |
| Padding | 13px top/bottom, 27px left/right |

### Tertiary (text link style)

For low-emphasis actions. Appears inline in body copy or in the footer action area.

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Color | `#5f8ec7` (Accent Blue) |
| Font | Trebuchet MS, Regular |
| Decoration | Underline |
| Usage | "Manage your preferences," "View order details," "Update your address" |

## 9. Email Component Standards

The following specs apply to all LEOptical email templates built in the MCA Brand editor and used throughout the course.

### Email container

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Maximum width | 600px |
| Background | `#ffffff` |
| Minimum top padding | 0 (the header block provides top spacing) |

### Header block

The header appears at the top of every email. It is not optional.

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Background color | `#11284f` (Navy) |
| Logo placement | Left-aligned, vertically centered |
| Logo max height | 36px |
| Padding | 20px top/bottom, 24px left/right |
| Nav links (if present) | Right-aligned, `#dbe7f4`, Arial Regular, 13px |

The header does not include a tagline or promotional copy. It is a structural element. If the email type requires a strong visual opener, that goes in the hero section below the header, not inside the header itself.

### Hero section

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Background | `#11284f` or full-bleed image with navy overlay |
| Text color | `#ffffff` |
| Heading | Georgia, Bold, 28px |
| Subhead (optional) | Trebuchet MS, Regular, 16px, White |
| Padding | 40px top/bottom, 24px left/right |
| CTA placement | Centered below subhead, 24px top margin |

The hero is present in promotional and loyalty emails. It is not used in transactional or health reminder emails, which lead with structured content instead.

### Body section

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Background | `#ffffff` |
| Text color | `#1e2a35` |
| Font | Trebuchet MS, Regular, 16px |
| Line height | 1.6 |
| Padding | 32px top/bottom, 24px left/right |
| Max paragraph width | 552px (600px container minus 24px padding each side) |

### Product block

Used in promotional emails to feature one or more products.

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Background | `#f7fafc` (Surface) |
| Border | 1px solid `rgba(17, 40, 79, 0.12)` |
| Border radius | 8px |
| Padding | 20px |
| Product image | Left column, 160px wide, 160px tall, object-fit: cover |
| Product name | Trebuchet MS, Bold, 18px, `#1e2a35` |
| Product description | Trebuchet MS, Regular, 15px, `#1e2a35` |
| Price (if shown) | Trebuchet MS, Bold, 16px, `#11284f` |
| CTA | Primary button (pill, navy), right-aligned or below description |
| Stacking on mobile | Image above text below. Image full-width at 320px breakpoint. |

Single-product blocks use a 2-column layout (image left, text right). Multi-product grids stack to a single column on mobile.

### Divider

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Color | `rgba(17, 40, 79, 0.12)` |
| Height | 1px |
| Margin | 24px top/bottom |
| Width | 100% of container |

### Footer block

The footer appears at the bottom of every email. It is not optional.

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Background | `#f7fafc` (Surface) |
| Text color | `#617084` (Muted) |
| Font | Arial, Regular, 12px |
| Line height | 1.5 |
| Padding | 24px top/bottom, 24px left/right |
| Unsubscribe link | Required in all promotional and loyalty emails. Color: `#617084`, underlined. |
| Address | LEOptical, 100 Vision Way, Suite 400, Austin, TX 78701 (placeholder) |
| Preference center link | "Manage your email preferences" above the unsubscribe link |

Footer content order (top to bottom):
1. Manage email preferences link
2. Unsubscribe link (for promotional and loyalty) or legal note (for transactional)
3. Physical mailing address
4. Copyright line

## 10. Product Family Messaging

Each product family has a distinct positioning statement and a two-line email description. Use these as the foundation for any email copy referencing a specific product. Do not alter the positioning statement. The email description can be adapted to fit the send context.

### Visionaire UltraLux (SKU: VIS-ULX-001)

**Positioning statement:** Precision lenses for everyday wear, light enough to forget you have them on.

**Email description:**
UltraLux frames are built for all-day use. Lightweight construction, UV400 protection, and optical-grade clarity without the bulk.

---

### Visionaire ChromaShift (SKU: VIS-CHS-001)

**Positioning statement:** Adaptive outdoor lenses that respond to light so you do not have to.

**Email description:**
ChromaShift lenses go from clear to polarized in seconds. Photochromic and polarized in one lens, no swapping required.

---

### SeeClear DailyFocus (SKU: SEC-DLF-001)

**Positioning statement:** Screen-tuned lenses that reduce fatigue for people who work in front of displays.

DailyFocus lenses filter blue light and correct for intermediate distance, the range most screens sit in. Designed for extended screen time without the end-of-day headache.

SunSync lenses block 100% of UV-A and UV-B outdoors and return to clear faster than standard photochromic lenses. Built for people who move between environments throughout the day.
